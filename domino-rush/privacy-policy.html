Domino Rush privacy policy

Effective 14 November 2026

Domino Rush is a game made by Zero8Eight Studio. This policy explains what the game does with information about you.

What the game collects

The game has no accounts and never asks for your name, email address, contacts or photos. To show ads, and to see how the game is played and where it goes wrong, it uses services from Google and Unity, which collect information about your device and how you use the game:

  • Ads, from Google AdMob. To show ads, count and pay for them and stop fraud, AdMob collects your device's advertising ID and app set ID, your IP address (and so roughly where you are, such as the city), how you use the game and its ads (launches, taps, videos watched) and diagnostic information such as how fast the game starts. Google may share it with the advertisers and ad networks that show ads through AdMob. How Google uses information from apps that use its services says more.
  • Ads, from Unity LevelPlay and Unity Ads. The game may show its ads through Unity's LevelPlay instead, which picks each ad from ad networks such as Unity Ads. To show ads, count and pay for them and stop fraud, LevelPlay and Unity Ads collect your device's advertising ID and other device identifiers, your IP address (and so roughly where you are), how you interact with the ads (views and taps), information about purchases made in apps, if any, and diagnostic information. Unity may share it with the advertisers and ad networks that show ads through LevelPlay. Unity's privacy policy for players says more.
  • Analytics, from Google's Firebase. To see how the game is played (runs started and finished, scores, which ads were watched), Firebase Analytics collects these game events, with an ID for this copy of the app, your device's model and operating system, and your country.
  • Crash reports, from Firebase Crashlytics. When the game crashes, Crashlytics sends a report of what went wrong, with the device's model, operating system and state, so we can fix it.
  • Settings, from Firebase Remote Config. The game fetches its current settings (such as how fast the wave runs, or how often ads play), sending your country, language and operating system so the right ones come back.

Google's privacy policy covers AdMob and Firebase, and Unity's privacy policy covers LevelPlay and Unity Ads.

Your choices

  • Consent: where the law asks for it, as in the European Union and the United Kingdom, the game asks first, through Google's consent form, whether ads (from Google or Unity) may use your data.
  • Personalised ads: in Android's settings (Settings › Privacy › Ads, or Settings › Google › Ads on some phones), you can delete or reset your advertising ID, or opt out of ads personalisation.
  • All of it: uninstalling the game stops all of this.

What stays on your phone

Your coins, best scores, missions, domino sets and settings (sound, music and vibration) are saved on your phone, so your progress is still there next time you play. They are never sent to us. Uninstalling the game deletes them.

Permissions

  • Vibration, for small buzzes as the dominoes fall. You can switch it off in the pause menu or the settings.
  • Internet and network state, to load the ads and send the information above.
  • Advertising ID, for the ads, as above.

Children

Domino Rush is not made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here, and the date at the top changes with it.
